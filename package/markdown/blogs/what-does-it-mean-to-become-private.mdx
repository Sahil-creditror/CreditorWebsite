Many entrepreneurs are exploring ways to organize their businesses, manage their affairs, protect their interests, and gain a better understanding of their legal and financial responsibilities. During this research, they often come across the term "becoming private."

But what does it actually mean to become private? Does it involve changing how a business is structured? Is it related to trusts, private membership associations, or business ownership? And how can entrepreneurs understand these concepts before making important decisions?

Becoming private is a broad term used in different educational and business contexts. It can refer to understanding private legal relationships, organizing business affairs, establishing appropriate agreements, and learning how private arrangements operate within applicable laws.

For entrepreneurs, the most important starting point is education. Before changing a business structure or creating a private arrangement, it is essential to understand the legal requirements, responsibilities, documentation, and potential consequences.

This practical guide explains the meaning of becoming private, the concepts commonly associated with it, the potential considerations for business owners, and how to approach the subject responsibly.

## Key Takeaways

- Becoming private is a broad concept, not a universal legal status that automatically changes a person's obligations.
- Entrepreneurs should understand the differences between private business arrangements and publicly registered business entities.
- Trusts, contracts, and private membership associations have different purposes and legal requirements.
- Proper documentation and responsible administration are important when establishing business arrangements.
- Private arrangements do not automatically eliminate taxes, licensing requirements, debts, or legal responsibilities.
- Business owners should seek qualified legal and tax advice before making structural changes.
- Learning about business organization and private arrangements can help entrepreneurs make more informed decisions.

## 1. What Does It Mean to Become Private?

The term "become private" is used to describe an approach to understanding and organizing certain personal, business, and legal affairs through private arrangements.

In an entrepreneurial context, it may involve learning about:
- Private agreements and contractual relationships
- Business ownership and organizational structures
- Trusts and fiduciary relationships
- Private membership associations
- Recordkeeping and business administration
- Rights and responsibilities under applicable laws
- The distinction between public-facing business activities and private contractual arrangements

However, there is an important distinction to understand: there is no single universal legal process that automatically makes an individual or business "private" or exempts them from public laws.

A business can use lawful private contracts and organizational arrangements while remaining subject to applicable federal, state, and local requirements.

For entrepreneurs, becoming more informed about private arrangements means understanding how these structures work, when they may be appropriate, and what responsibilities come with them.

### Why Are Entrepreneurs Interested in Becoming Private?

Business owners may explore these concepts for several reasons:

1. **Understanding business ownership**: Entrepreneurs want to understand who owns a business, who manages it, and how ownership and decision-making responsibilities are documented.
2. **Learning about different organizational structures**: Some business owners want to understand the differences between LLCs, corporations, partnerships, trusts, and other arrangements.
3. **Improving business organization**: Proper agreements, records, and administrative procedures can help establish clarity in business relationships.
4. **Understanding private contractual relationships**: Entrepreneurs may want to learn how contracts and membership agreements define relationships between parties.
5. **Planning for long-term business goals**: Business owners may explore ownership arrangements, succession planning, and family legacy considerations as their businesses grow.

## 2. Public vs. Private: Understanding the Difference

One of the first concepts entrepreneurs should understand is the difference between public-facing business activities and private arrangements.

The terms "public" and "private" can have different meanings depending on the legal context. They should not be treated as two completely separate legal worlds.

For example, a business may enter into private contracts with customers and suppliers while also maintaining public registrations, filing required reports, and complying with government regulations.

### Example

Imagine an entrepreneur operates an LLC that provides consulting services.

The business may have:
- State registration documents
- An operating agreement
- Private agreements with clients
- Internal policies
- Business banking accounts
- Required licenses or professional registrations

The state registration may be part of the public record, while client agreements and internal operating documents may remain private unless disclosure is legally required.

This demonstrates that public registration and private contractual relationships can exist together.

## 3. Understanding the Role of Business Structures

Business structure is an important part of understanding private business arrangements.

The structure you choose can influence ownership, management, taxation, liability, reporting, and administrative responsibilities.

Common business structures include:
- **Sole Proprietorship**: Operated by one individual without creating a separate state-law entity.
- **Limited Liability Company (LLC)**: Business entity created under state law providing legal separation between business and owners.
- **Corporation**: Separate legal entity with formal governance (shareholders, directors, officers, bylaws).
- **Partnership**: Two or more people carrying on a business together under a partnership agreement.
- **Trust**: Legal arrangement where a trustee holds and manages property for beneficiaries.

## 4. What Is the Role of a Business Trust?

Business trusts are often discussed in educational material about private business arrangements. A business trust is generally an arrangement involving property or business interests held and managed by a trustee according to the trust document.

### Important Considerations

A trust does not automatically protect every asset from creditors, remove tax obligations, or eliminate legal responsibilities. Its effectiveness depends on the trust terms, applicable law, proper administration, timing, and the circumstances involved.

## 5. What Is a Private Membership Association?

A Private Membership Association (PMA) is an arrangement through which members participate in an association governed by its membership terms and applicable law.

Before establishing or joining a PMA, business owners should consider whether activities are lawful, whether membership terms are documented, licensing/registration rules, consumer protection, and taxes.

## 6. How Does Becoming Private Relate to Business Operations?

Understanding private arrangements is only one part of running a business. Entrepreneurs also need reliable operational systems:
- **Business Documentation**: Maintain formation documents, ownership records, operating agreements, contracts, and minutes.
- **Business Banking**: Use suitable business banking and avoid mixing personal and business funds.
- **Website and Merchant Processing**: Identify the business accurately with clear terms, refund policies, and secure payments.
- **Financial Recordkeeping**: Maintain accurate records of income, expenses, invoices, and payments.

## 8. Common Misconceptions About Becoming Private

- Becoming private does NOT mean government laws no longer apply.
- A trust does NOT automatically protect all business assets unconditionally.
- A PMA does NOT eliminate all licensing requirements.
- Private businesses still have tax responsibilities.
- Becoming private does NOT guarantee financial freedom.
- An LLC and a trust are NOT interchangeable.
- Private agreements cannot override mandatory legal requirements.
